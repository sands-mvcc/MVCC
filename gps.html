How Speech GPS works

Your outline is a GPS for your speech. Use this tool after you have your outline and research. It turns them into a route you can see, check, and rehearse.

Old GPS versus modern GPS

Printed directions = manuscript outline

  • Fully written, word for word
  • Eyes off the road
  • Driving distraction

Modern GPS = keyword outline

  • More graphics, fewer words
  • Eyes on the road
  • Less distraction

The same rules apply to your outline: fewer words, more images, never read word for word, keep eye contact and move about, and use a conversational style.

Plan your trip in four steps

  1. Plan: set your destination (what the audience will remember) and finish the pre-trip check.
  2. Route: start with your introduction, add each main point as a stop, add points of interest, and signal every turn with a transition.
  3. Road check: fix red closures and orange slowdowns. Long lines mean your eyes leave the road.
  4. Directions: press Start to rehearse with turn-by-turn prompts and a timer. Arrow keys, Space, or a presentation clicker move to the next turn.

Quotations: less is more

You can say more than the audience sees. Put only the key words on your card.

On your card: Dr. David Stout, BCC Pres. “Brookdale committed: affordable/accessible education. All Mon Cty residents” Brookdalecc.edu 11/26
Not on your card: the full sentence, word for word, with the full web address and update date.

Map legend

  • Start: your introduction
  • Numbered stop: a main point
  • Turn: a transition
  • Scenic view: a visual aid
  • Landmark: a quotation
  • Evidence: a fact or statistic
  • Story or example
  • Challenge ahead: your backup plan
  • Road clear
  • Slow traffic: something to fix
  • Road closed: must fix
