Help & troubleshooting

Check my setup

Runs a quick check of this browser and your devices, then tells you what to fix.

How do I hand in my speech?
  1. Add your name under Your details.
  2. Record your take, score yourself, and add coaching if your instructor asks for it.
  3. In step 5, choose Download submission (.zip).
  4. Upload that one .zip file to your course assignment. Don't unzip it first.

Want to hand in an earlier take instead? Open My takes, open that take, and choose its Download submission button.

The camera won't turn on, or I never saw a permission pop-up

Your browser or your computer is blocking access. Work through these in order.

In your browser
  1. Chrome or Edge: click the camera or lock icon at the left of the address bar, set Camera and Microphone to Allow, then reload.
  2. Safari: Safari › Settings › Websites, choose Camera and then Microphone, and set this site to Allow.
  3. Firefox: click the camera icon in the address bar, clear any blocked setting, then reload.
On a Mac
  1. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security › Camera, and turn on your browser.
  2. Do the same under Microphone.
  3. Quit your browser completely and reopen it.
On Windows
  1. Open Settings › Privacy & security › Camera. Turn on camera access and allow desktop apps.
  2. Repeat under Microphone, then reload this page.
It says the camera is busy, or the picture is black

Another program is almost certainly holding your camera. Zoom, Teams, and Meet keep it reserved even when hidden.

  1. Quit those apps completely — closing the window often isn't enough.
  2. Reload this page and turn the camera on again.

A physical privacy shutter or a laptop camera-off key can also cause a black picture.

The mic level bar doesn't move
  • Check for a hardware mute — many headsets have an inline switch.
  • With Bluetooth earbuds, try your computer's built-in mic instead.
  • Make sure the right microphone is selected in your system sound settings.
  • Turn the camera off and on again after changing any of this.
No words appear in the transcript

Live captions work in Chrome and Edge, not Safari or Firefox. Record in Chrome or Edge, or keep recording where you are and type or paste your words into the transcript box, then choose Recount.

Captions also need an internet connection, and they stop if you switch windows mid-take.

I need to present slides while I record

Start recording here first, then switch to your slides. Timing, pauses, and energy are measured from the audio, so they stay accurate. Live captions stop while this tab is hidden — paste your words into the transcript box afterwards and choose Recount.

The transcript got my words wrong

That's expected, and it isn't a judgement on how you speak. Edit the transcript box, then choose Recount. Always fix the transcript before trusting the filler-word count.

Where does my video go? Can anyone see it?

Your recordings are saved in this browser's own storage on your device. They aren't uploaded, and nobody — including your instructor — sees them unless you hand them in.

One exception: live captions send audio to your browser's built-in speech service. For fully private practice, turn captions off and type your transcript.

On a shared or lab computer, choose Clear history under My takes before you leave.

My saved takes disappeared

Takes are stored in this browser on this computer only. They won't follow you to another machine or browser, and they're erased if you clear browsing data or use private browsing. Use Download all takes to keep your own copy.

My file is too big to hand in

Before you record, open Set up the take and change Recording quality to 480p or Audio only. Audio only is dramatically smaller and every measurement still works. If you only need the report, use Print or save as PDF or Copy report.

I don't have a Claude account

You don't need one. In step 4, pick whichever assistant you have — Copilot, ChatGPT, Gemini, or something else. Check whether your school already provides one before signing up.

Can I use my phone or tablet?

It usually records, but a laptop or desktop in Chrome or Edge is much better — captions are more reliable and there's room to work. On a phone, record short takes and expect to type your transcript.

What good sounds like — reference points

Rough pictures of common delivery styles — not targets to hit. Excellent speakers often sit outside any band on purpose.

A clear informative talk
Pace around 130–150 wpm, few fillers, steady energy with gentle variation

Easy to follow, calm authority. The energy rises and falls a little to mark what matters.

An energetic persuasive speech
Pace often 150–180 wpm, strong energy variation, frequently builds toward the end

If your take is fast but clear and full of energy, read a “moving quickly” flag as a style choice to control, not a failure.

A flat read — what to lift
Very level energy, flat or fading arc, words often technically fine

Usually a sign of reading word-for-word. The fix isn’t speaking louder — it’s lifting your voice on the lines you care about and looking up between them.

For instructors

Create an assignment link

Set up the take in Set up the take above, give it a title here, then build a link. Every student who opens it lands on the same setup, and the title is stamped on every submission. Nothing is stored on a server — the assignment travels inside the link.

Collect submissions

Select the .zip files students handed in — or download them all from your LMS and select them together. You get one table with every student's numbers and a class summary to download. Files are read in this browser only.

Video check: each submission records a SHA-256 fingerprint of its video. “Matches” means the video in the zip is the one the numbers were measured from. It catches swapped or re-edited videos, not a student who edits both files on purpose — treat it as a consistency check, not proof.
