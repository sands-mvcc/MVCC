    Design work students can’t hand to a chatbot.

    Generative AI can draft a passable essay, lab report, or summary in seconds. This activity helps you redesign assignments so the learning, and the evidence of it, has to come from your students.

    “Write a 1,000-word essay explaining the causes of major data breaches.”
    redesigned with four tactics
    AI-resistant version
    Investigate the causes of data breaches by:
    • Picking one breach notice from an organization in our county (local case study)
    • Submitting a sources memo and outline before drafting (process scaffolding)
    • Mapping the causes to a system you use at work or school (personalization)
    • Defending your top recommendation in a 4-minute conference (synchronous)

    How this activity works

    1. Rate yourselfCheck your starting confidence on eight teacher competencies below.
    2. Study the tacticsOpen each of the 13 methods, read the examples, and pass its quick check.
    3. Redesign an assignmentCombine tactics in the lab and watch resistance and workload change.
    4. Check and reflectTake the 10-question knowledge check, then commit to a plan for your own course.
    An honest starting point. No assignment is AI-proof, and AI detectors are not reliable enough to carry an integrity decision on their own. The goal here is to make AI shortcuts less useful than doing the work, and to collect evidence of learning you can trust.

    Concepts and competencies teachers need

    These eight competencies, grouped into four domains, are what an instructor needs to limit inappropriate AI use without policing students. Rate your confidence on each; your ratings are saved to the progress log.
