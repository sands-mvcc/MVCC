The Age of Revolutions

Between 1640 and 1850, four political revolutions and one industrial one remade the Atlantic world. Kings lost power, enslaved people seized freedom, factories rewrote the working day, and new ideologies argued over who deserved rights.

Who gets to be free and equal, and who decides?

Big ideas

Revolutions spread through the Atlantic world; each one widened the meaning of “rights” and also drew a boundary around who held them. Industrialization created new classes and new grievances, and the ideologies of the nineteenth century were arguments about how to answer them.

Lesson path

    Instructor notes

    Pacing: two 50-minute sessions or one 100-minute block. Part 1 (revolutions) takes about 50 minutes; Part 2 (industry and ideologies) about 35; Part 3 (assessment and reflection) about 15.

    Alignment: C3 Framework D2.His.1.9-12 (context), D2.His.14.9-12 (multiple causes and effects), D2.His.16.9-12 (evidence-based argument); AP World History Units 5 and 6.

    Formative checks: the comparison matrix, sorting task, and connection map are auto-checked; the radicalism ranking and reflection prompts are open-ended and work well for pair discussion or a short written response.

    Differentiation: students can use the glossary at any point; the timeline filters let struggling readers focus on one revolution at a time. For extension, ask students to add a fifth case (Latin American independence, 1810–1825) to the matrix.

    Data note: population and coal figures in the industrialization scene are rounded historical estimates; sources vary.
