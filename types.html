    Every business is built on a legal choice

    Before the logo, the website or the first customer, an owner decides what the business legally is. That one decision sets who pays when things go wrong, how profit is taxed, who gets a vote and how money can be raised.

    What you will be able to do

    By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

    1. Describe the seven common ownership arrangements and how each is formed.
    2. Compare structures across liability, taxation, control and funding.
    3. Predict whose assets are exposed when a business can’t pay a debt or loses a lawsuit.
    4. Estimate how pass-through and corporate taxation change what an owner keeps.
    5. Recommend and defend a structure for a realistic business scenario.

    How these numbers are calculated

    Illustrative flat rates so the structures can be compared: 22% personal income tax, 21% federal corporate tax, 15% qualified dividend tax. Pass-through owners pay self-employment tax of 15.3% on 92.35% of profit (Social Security portion capped at $176,100, the 2025 wage base; Medicare 2.9% uncapped), and deduct half of it. The S corp owner is assumed to take half the profit as a reasonable salary subject to payroll tax. State taxes, the qualified business income deduction and progressive brackets are left out.

    Use this to see the shape of the trade-off, never to make a real filing decision.
