Cyber attacks

In 2000 a former contractor used radio equipment to send commands to sewage pumping stations in Maroochy Shire, Australia, causing hundreds of thousands of liters of sewage spills. Remote lift stations remain a soft target.

Defenses

Try the attacks with the high-level float failed as well. The hardwired float is often the last thing between an attack and a sewage spill.

How pretreatment works

Pretreatment (the headworks) removes material that would damage or clog equipment downstream and measures and characterizes what is arriving. It changes BOD very little, but a plant without it fails quickly.

Lift station

Gravity sewers end deep underground, so a lift station collects flow in a wet well and pumps it up to the headworks. Submersible pumps on variable-frequency drives (VFDs) are typical. A remote terminal unit (RTU) runs a level loop: as level rises above setpoint, pumps speed up. Lead/lag logic starts more pumps as demand rises and rotates the lead pump to even out wear.

Because a failure means sewage in streets and streams, stations carry hardwired backups: a high-level float that forces pumps on, and a low-level float that stops them before they run dry. A sanitary sewer overflow (SSO) is a reportable violation under the Clean Water Act.

Screening

Mechanically cleaned fine screens (often 6 mm openings) catch rags, wipes, plastics and debris. As material builds up, the screen blinds and the water level upstream rises above the level downstream. The PLC runs the rake on a timer and whenever differential level (ΔP) exceeds setpoint. Screenings are washed and compacted to reduce weight and odor before disposal.

Headloss rises roughly with the square of flow and with blinding: ΔP ∝ (Q ÷ open area)²

If screens cannot keep up, water overtops into a bypass channel with a coarse manual bar rack. Rags that pass foul pumps, mixers, diffusers and digesters for months.

Influent quality protection

A pH or conductivity analyzer on the influent gives early warning of illegal industrial discharges. Automatic diversion to an emergency basin protects the biological process, and the stored flow is bled back slowly once the event passes.

Grit removal

Grit (sand, gravel, coffee grounds, eggshells) is heavier than organic solids. In a vortex grit chamber, a paddle maintains a circular flow; grit spirals to the center hopper while lighter organics stay in suspension. A grit pump sends the slurry to a cyclone and a screw classifier that washes and dewaters it. Grit that escapes wears pumps and centrifuges and fills digesters and channels.

Removal efficiency falls at high flows, and storms deliver huge grit loads in the first flush, which is why operators increase grit pumping ahead of wet weather.

Flow, sampling and odor

A Parshall flume with an ultrasonic level sensor measures influent flow. That flow paces the composite sampler, which takes a small aliquot every set volume and holds it refrigerated at 6 °C or below. These samples are the legal basis for removal efficiency reporting.

Headworks release hydrogen sulfide, a toxic and corrosive gas. Fans draw air through a chemical scrubber or biofilter. Gas detectors protect workers; H₂S above about 10 ppm calls for action, and at high concentrations it deadens the sense of smell.

Remote sites such as lift stations often reach SCADA over radio or cellular links. Those links must be authenticated and encrypted, or anyone with a radio can send commands, as Maroochy Shire showed.

Guided exercises
  1. Run a full day at 20×. When do the lag pumps start? Compare pump power at night and at the afternoon peak.
  2. Start a storm. Track wet well level, the number of pumps running and screen ΔP. Did anything overflow?
  3. During the storm’s first flush, watch the grit hopper. Raise grit pumping until the hopper stops climbing.
  4. Trigger a wipes surge. Watch pump amps rise as rags build up, then run a de-rag cycle.
  5. Take channel B out of service during a wipes surge. When does the bypass start, and how many rags get through?
  6. Trigger an acid dump with automatic diversion on, then with it off. Compare acid minutes passed.
  7. Launch the radio pump-stop attack. What stopped the overflow? Repeat with the high-level float failed.
  8. Complete three mystery fault challenges.
