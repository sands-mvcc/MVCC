Fill in your outline, pick one of twenty instructional graphic types, and this studio writes a complete, well-structured prompt for an AI image or code tool. Then review, export, and spin off variations.

Prepare the lesson content

The AI can only draw what you give it. Start from your lesson outline: the handout title, the items students must tell apart, and the points you want compared. The example below is a business course comparing types of ownership; replace it with your own topic.

    Why this step matters

    Image models fill gaps with plausible-sounding text. A precise list of items and points keeps the AI working from your content instead of inventing its own, and it gives you a checklist to verify against later.

      Review the generated handout

        4

        Download and use the graphic

        Save the image as PNG (best for sharp text) or JPEG. If you used the code-rendered route, open the HTML file in a browser and print to PDF. Then choose where it is going.

        5

        Ask for variations

        Stay in the same chat so the AI keeps your content, then ask for new versions. One verified handout can become a poster, a worksheet, a quiz, and a version for English learners.

        Interpret first

        Use the handout as a warm-up: pairs study it for three minutes and write two questions it raises before you teach.

        Run an error hunt

        Hand out a version with two planted mistakes. Students who catch them are practicing the same verification habit you used in step 3.

        Cross the curriculum

        Nothing here is business-specific. Swap the outline for cell organelles, causes of a war, or the OSI layers and rebuild.
