The AI Assessment Scale (AIAS)

The AI Assessment Scale provides an evidence-based, transparent standard to calibrate how Generative AI tools are permitted or mandated across tasks. Rather than an all-or-nothing ban, the 5-point spectrum clarifies cognitive accountability, protects authentic learning outcomes, and builds student AI literacy.

The 5 Core AIAS Levels

Select any level below to review educator guidance, permissible tools, verification prompts, and student declaration phrasing.

Spectrum: Restrictive → Transformative

Alignment with Learning Objectives

Never choose an AIAS level randomly. If the core objective is mastering foundational synthesis or syntactic recall, use Level 1 (No AI). If the objective is higher-order critical evaluation or system design, employ Level 4 or 5.

Constructive Alignment (Biggs)

Process Transparency Over Penalization

AI detection software yields catastrophic false positives and punishes non-native English speakers. AIAS replaces punitive guessing with explicit Student Acknowledgement Statements and prompt-log audits.

Authentic Assessment Protocol

Dynamic Task Calibration

A course rarely sits on one static level. A semester starts with Level 1 to establish baseline competence, moves to Level 2/3 for research and structural outlining, and reaches Level 4/5 for real-world capstone synthesis.

Decision Rule of Thumb for Educators:

Level 1 (No AI): Memory, unassisted coding syntax, in-person exam, moral reflection.
Level 2 (Idea Gen): Overcoming blank-page paralysis, structuring thesis angles, identifying counters.
Level 3 (Edit/Polish): Grammar coaching, formatting citations, debugging student's original code.
Level 4 (AI Partner): Co-writing, translating, simulating interviews, iterative prototype building.
Level 5 (Full AI): Critiquing AI output, prompt engineering mastery, detecting algorithmic bias.
