The AI Assessment Scale (AIAS v2.1)

The updated AIAS moves definitively away from unenforceable AI detection and policing. It reimagines the 5-point spectrum as an assessment redesign methodology where teachers calibrate authentic tasks, required evidence, and evaluation rubrics to match the explicit cognitive role of AI.

Non-Hierarchical Level 1 is not "punishment" and Level 5 is not "the goal". Every level is equally valid.
Redesign Over Policing AI text detectors are unreliable. Authenticity is achieved through task design and multimodal proof.
Unified Descriptors Condensed single-statement guidelines designed to plug directly into student assignment briefs.

What Changed Between AIAS v1 and AIAS v2.1?

Key architectural adjustments made to reflect generative AI realities in classrooms

Tier Legacy AIAS (v1) Updated AIAS (v2.1) Core Pedagogical Rationale & Assessment Redesign
Level 1 No AI (Prohibited entirely) No AI (Controlled Environment) Recognizes that take-home bans cannot be policed. Level 1 is only pedagogically valid in proctored, invigilated, in-person, or air-gapped environments.
Level 2 AI Ideas & Structure (Brainstorming) AI Planning (Topic Exploration) Shifts away from restrictive "rulebook" language. Defines pre-writing inquiry, exploratory prompting, and structural scaffolding as legitimate parts of the research process.
Level 3 AI Editing (Grammar, syntax) AI Collaboration (Drafting & Feedback) Eliminates the false assumption that text modification can be checked with detectors. Embraces iterative drafting with custom GPTs, conversational co-writing, and rubric critique.
Level 4 AI + Human Evaluation (Human critiques) Full AI (Directed AI Critical Thinking) Students direct AI to produce complete outputs. The grading rubric assesses the student's orchestration: their prompt strategies, verification checks, bias audits, and editorial critique.
Level 5 Full AI (Unrestricted co-creation) AI Exploration (Creative Problem-Solving) Elevates AI to a mandatory tool of digital literacy. The assessment task is explicitly constructed so that it is impossible or impractical to solve without advanced generative AI workflows.

AIAS v2.1 Educator Decision Framework:

Level 1: Controlled Must occur in invigilated, closed-book, or oral settings. Unenforceable asynchronously.
Level 2: Planning AI aids research scope, outlines, and inquiry questions. Substantive drafting is human-driven.
Level 3: Collaboration Conversational interaction with AI assistants, code debugging, or custom roleplay bots.
Level 4: Full AI (Critical) AI generates whole artifacts; marking criteria explicitly evaluates student critique and verification.
Level 5: AI Exploration Complex real-world innovation where AI toolchains are mandatory to complete the challenge.
