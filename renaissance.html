Renaissance and Reformation

Between about 1350 and 1600, Europeans went back to ancient sources to find better models for living, painting, and believing. The same habit of questioning that made the Renaissance also cracked the unity of Western Christianity.

Who has the authority to say what is true: tradition, the text, or the individual?

Big ideas

Humanists taught Europeans to return to original sources and test them. Printing put those sources, and arguments about them, in thousands of hands. When reformers applied the same method to the Church, the result was a crisis of authority that princes and popes then fought over.

    Humanism: back to the sources

    Humanists were scholars who believed that studying the literature of ancient Greece and Rome would produce wiser, more eloquent, more virtuous people. Their motto was ad fontes, “to the sources.” Most were devout Christians: they wanted to renew their world, not abandon faith.

    Ad fontesRead the original texts, in the original languages, and correct the errors that crept in over centuries of copying.
    Studia humanitatisA new curriculum of grammar, rhetoric, poetry, history, and moral philosophy, designed to train active citizens.
    Human dignity and potentialPeople can shape their own lives through learning and effort. The ideal was the many-talented “universal man.”
    Civic humanismIn republics like Florence, learning served public life. The best life was one of service to the city.
    “We have made you neither of heaven nor of earth, neither mortal nor immortal, so that you may, as the free and proud shaper of your own being, fashion yourself in whatever form you prefer.”Pico della Mirandola, Oration on the Dignity of Man, 1486 (God speaking to Adam)
    Read closely: what does Pico claim makes humans unique, and why might a Church that stressed human sinfulness find this idea both inspiring and unsettling?

    Art lab: how painters made the world look real

    Renaissance artists, funded by patrons like the Medici and the popes, used mathematics, anatomy, and close observation to make painted space and bodies look real. Their techniques expressed humanist confidence that the natural world and the human form were worth studying.

    Linear perspective

    Drag the red point, or select it and use the arrow keys.

    Around 1415–1420 the architect Filippo Brunelleschi showed that parallel lines going into the distance can be drawn to meet at a single vanishing point on the horizon line, which sits at the viewer’s eye level. Masaccio used the system in his Holy Trinity fresco around 1427.

    Chiaroscuro and sfumato

    Chiaroscuro (“light-dark”) models form with strong contrasts of light and shadow. Sfumato (“smoky”), Leonardo’s specialty, softens edges so forms melt into shadow.

    Why print mattered for the Reformation

    Speed. Luther’s Ninety-five Theses were posted in October 1517; reprinted copies circulated across Germany within weeks. By some estimates, Luther’s writings made up about a third of German-language publications in the early 1520s.
    Vernacular scripture. Erasmus’s Greek New Testament (1516) let scholars check the Latin Vulgate. Luther’s German New Testament (1522) let ordinary readers judge church teaching against scripture for themselves.
    Images for everyone. Cheap woodcut pamphlets carried arguments to people who could not read, often mocking the pope or praising Luther.

    Why did the Reformation happen?

    Earlier critics such as John Wycliffe and Jan Hus had challenged the Church, and Hus was burned at the stake in 1415. Luther’s protest in 1517 succeeded where theirs failed because four forces converged. Open each cause to see the evidence.

      The Catholic Reformation
